# Pricing playbook

## The four numbers, in this order

1. **Floor**: hours to deliver, including revisions and admin, multiplied by the minimum rate the user will accept. Below this, decline the job.
2. **Anchor**: the highest comparable price found in live listings or public pricing pages, with link and date.
3. **Value**: what the outcome is worth to the buyer per month. Only use figures the buyer stated or that are publicly visible. If neither exists, skip value pricing and price on scope.
4. **The number**: sits between the anchor and the value, never below the floor, never a round guess.

## Structuring options

Three options beat one number, because the buyer chooses how to buy rather than whether to buy.

- Essential: the narrow fix, fastest, lowest price. Deliberately missing one thing they will want.
- Complete: the recommended option. Mark it. Should be 2 to 3 times Essential.
- Complete plus support: Complete plus months of retainer. This is where recurring income starts.

Anchor high by listing the most expensive option first in the table body only if the channel supports a table. In chat and DMs, lead with the recommended one.

## Payment terms

- Default: 50 percent deposit, balance on completion. No deposit, no start.
- Above three weeks of work: 40 / 30 / 30 across milestones.
- Retainers: monthly in advance, 30 day notice, price review at 6 months.
- Marketplaces: use escrow milestones. Never work outside the platform's protection.
- Always name a price expiry date. It converts more than any discount.

## Discounting

Never discount the price. Change the scope instead. If the buyer needs a lower number:

- Remove a deliverable and show the new price
- Extend the timeline in exchange for a lower fee
- Trade price for a case study, a testimonial on camera, or two named referrals
- Offer the paid diagnostic as the entry point

## Paid diagnostic

A small fixed fee, delivered in days, that produces a document the buyer keeps. It qualifies the buyer, gets money moving, and turns the big proposal into a second conversation rather than a cold one. Price it at 5 to 10 percent of the main project and credit it against the project if they proceed within 30 days.

## Currency and cross-border

- Quote in the buyer's currency where possible.
- State the conversion rate and the date used when converting.
- Add a buffer for platform fees, payment processing and transfer costs before quoting. Marketplace fees are commonly around 10 percent, so check the current rate on the platform and build it in.
- For long projects across currencies, fix the rate in the terms or price in the stronger currency.

## Retainer design

- Price the retainer on the outcome and the response commitment, not on hours.
- Cap the scope with a monthly ceiling, for example "up to X items or Y hours, unused time does not roll over".
- Put a 90 day minimum on it, then monthly.
- Build in a quarterly review where the price can move up.
