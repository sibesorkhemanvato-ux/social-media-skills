# Objection handling

One rule under all of these: answer in two or three lines, then ask a question. Never argue, never re-pitch the whole proposal.

## "Too expensive"

Find out which of the three it really is: no budget, no perceived value, or a cheaper quote in hand.

> Fair. Which part is the issue, the total or the timing of it? If it is the total, I can take out [deliverable] and it becomes [price]. If it is timing, the same scope split over two months works.

## "We have a cheaper quote"

> Likely so. The difference is usually [named scope item] and who carries the [risk]. Send me their scope and I will tell you honestly if it covers the same ground. If it does, take it.

## "Send me something and we will get back to you"

> Happy to. So it does not sit unread, I will follow up on [date]. If it is a no by then, tell me and I will close the file.

## "We want to do it in house"

> Often the right call. The part teams usually get stuck on is [specific step]. I can do just that piece for [price] and hand your team the rest documented.

## "No budget right now"

> Understood. When does the next budget cycle open? In the meantime here is the one change you can make for free: [specific tip]. I will check back on [date].

## "How do we know you can do it?"

> You do not yet. That is what the first milestone is for: [small deliverable] for [price], and if it is not right you stop there and owe nothing further.

## "Can you start now?"

> Yes, the [date] slot is open. It holds until [date] once the deposit clears. Anything that starts later moves to [next date].

## "We need to talk internally"

> Makes sense. Who else needs to see this, and what will they want to know? I will write that part so you are not defending it alone.

## Silence

Three touches, then close the file politely. The day 14 message is the one that most often gets a reply, because it removes the pressure.

## Never do these

- Never drop the price unprompted after silence
- Never send a fourth follow-up in the same cycle
- Never imply scarcity that is not real
- Never bad-mouth the competing supplier
- Never accept a scope change without a written price change
